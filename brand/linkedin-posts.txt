=== A) Question hook ===

10 new customers walked into your shop this month.

How many came back?

Most local shop owners don't know. Not because they don't care, but because nobody is keeping count, and nobody is reminding the customer to come back.

Big chains fixed this years ago with loyalty apps. Local shops tried too:

❌ Paper stamp cards: lost in a wallet, forgotten at home
❌ Apps: "Please download our app" … and the customer is already out the door
❌ QR codes and points: nobody wants that with a queue at the counter

So we built Regularhood, a stamp card that lives on WhatsApp.

The customer says their phone number.
The shop taps "Add visit".
That's it.

Every 5th visit → a free gift 🎁 (the shop picks the number and the reward)
And WhatsApp sends a nudge: "Just 1 more visit!"

No app for the customer. No QR. No paper. Free, with a 1-minute setup.

It works for any shop people come back to: cafés, salons, bakeries, kirana stores, gyms.

=== B) Build-in-public ===

Our first version had points, QR codes, PINs and bill tracking.

We deleted almost all of it.

Here's why.

We were building a loyalty program for local shops: salons, bakeries, kirana stores, gyms. Every feature made sense on a whiteboard.

Then we watched a real counter at rush hour.

The person at the counter has about 5 seconds per customer. There's a queue. Someone is asking for change. Nobody is scanning a QR code or explaining how points work.

So we asked one question of every feature:
"Can someone learn this in 10 seconds, with a queue in front of them?"

Most features failed.

What survived:
→ The customer says their phone number
→ The shop taps "Add visit"
→ Every 5th visit is a free gift 🎁
→ The stamp card reaches the customer on WhatsApp

One keypad. One button. The screen turns orange when a reward is due.

That's Regularhood. Free, unlimited customers, works on any phone.

👉 www.regularhood.in

The lesson I keep relearning: adding features is easy. Deleting them takes courage.
