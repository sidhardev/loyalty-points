Starbucks has a loyalty app.
Your favourite neighbourhood café has a barista who remembers your order.

Guess which one is losing customers to the new café that opened down the street.

Every independent café survives on regulars.
The person who orders the same cold brew every morning.
The freelancer who works from the corner table three days a week.
The couple who come in every Sunday for brunch.

But "regular" is a feeling, not a system.
And a feeling won't stop someone from trying the new place with the "Grand Opening – 20% OFF" sign.

Chains solved this years ago. Independent cafés tried too:

❌ Paper stamp cards → lost in a wallet, forgotten at home
❌ Loyalty apps → "Could you download our app?" … and the customer has already left
❌ QR codes, points, PINs → nobody wants that with a queue at the counter

So we built a loyalty app too. Our first version had points, QR codes, PINs and bill tracking.

Then we deleted almost all of it.

What's left is the simplest loyalty program you can build. On purpose.

Introducing Regularhood

The customer says their phone number.
The barista taps "Add visit".
Done.

Every 5th visit → a free cappuccino. (The café picks the number and the reward.)

✅ No app for the customer
✅ No QR code
✅ No points maths
✅ No sign-up

The stamp card reaches the customer on WhatsApp.

Built for a busy counter during the morning rush:
→ One keypad. One button.
→ The screen turns orange when a reward is due
→ A ready "we miss you" WhatsApp for customers who haven't been back in a while
→ A counter poster and Instagram/WhatsApp status image, made for you
→ Works on any phone

And it's free. Unlimited customers.

👉 Try it free: https://www.regularhood.in

The biggest lesson from building this:
the hard part isn't adding features. It's having the courage to delete them.

If a barista can't learn it in 10 seconds, it doesn't exist.
